Act one

Working out what you believe

The hard part isn't the swiping, it's capturing a person's moral taste without ever asking them an ethics question. Four screens do it, and the user experiences none of them as a test.

Act two

Deciding together

Votes stay blind until both are in, so neither person anchors the other. But disagreement isn't smoothed away — it's named, because an app that only finds agreement is just a shared watchlist.

One decision still open

Bars or map — and a number decides it, not taste.

The compass and overlap screens exist in both forms. If factor analysis finds two dimensions carrying most of the spread, the map wins and the shortlist becomes visibly the films sitting in the gap between two people. If the top two factors explain little, the plane is a flattering lie and the bars are the honest choice. The map is the prettier screen. That is not a reason.

Open

What still needs deciding

  1. A one-line moral premise per film doesn't exist yet. It's the most important string in the product — what makes a card readable in two seconds — and nothing currently generates it.
  2. The axis labels are placeholders. They stand in for whatever the factor analysis names. Neither the bars nor the map care how many factors there are.
  3. Is the “why you're seeing this” line insight or clutter? It's a toggle in the source. Judge it switched off.
  4. No post-watch screen. “Did it land?” after the credits is the cheapest correction signal there is, and it would close the loop on the profile.
  5. Pass-the-phone is one card, not a flow. Two people on one couch is probably the common case and currently has the least design.
  6. Nothing is designed for empty states — a person far from everyone, or a pair with no overlap. It will happen.
